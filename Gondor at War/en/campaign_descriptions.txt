{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_ENGLAND_TITLE}Mordor
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_ENGLAND_DESCR}Leader: Sauron\nHeir: Witch-king\nCapital: Barad-dûr\n\n\n\n"Ash nazg durbatulûk, ash nazg gimbatul, ash nazg thrakatulûk, agh burzum-ishi krimpatul." - Ring inscription\n\nStarting Location:\nThe realm of Mordor is, geographically, isolated. Mountains protect the heartlands on three sides and allies cover the gap to the east. This gives Mordor a very safe early game.\n\nEarly Game:\nThere are not many rebel regions for Mordor to expand into before they must face an enemy. Gondor already shares a border with you at the start of the game and will press for Minas Morgul. To the north, you can claim some of the deserted lands between Udûn and Mirkwood but your vassal; Dol Guldur, may try to take them also Applying pressure to Gondor as soon as possible is advised.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nMordor is a faction of many talents but they focus on cheap, poor-quality, Orcs. With overwhelming numbers, your armies will pin down their enemy whilst the various elite units you have deal the killing blow. Your armies have poor morale, poor armour quality and poor weapon quality but vast numbers and powerful generals. The Nazgûl and their Dark Númenórean entourage will keep your hordes fighting long after the enemy has fled in fear.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Respawning Nazgûl\n- Sauron’s Return (One Ring Mechanic)\n\nRare Units:\n- Great Beasts (Mordor Regions)\n- Dark Númenóreans (Durthang)\n- Olog-hai (Mordor Regions)
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_TEUTONIC_ORDER_TITLE}Enedwaith
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_TEUTONIC_ORDER_DESCR}Leader: Moot-Speaker Tremac\nHeir: Chieftain Harec\nCapital: Alcfud\n\n\n\n"A fairly numerous but barbarous fisher-folk dwelt between the mouths of the Gwathló and the Angren... tribes of 'Wild Men', fishers and fowlers, on the coasts of Enedwaith, who were akin in race and speech to Drúedain of Anórien." - Little known background of the clans\n\nStarting Location:\nEnedwaith is bounded to the north by the Gwathló river; to the west by the ocean; to the south by the White Mountains and; to the east by the Misty Mountains. This does give the clans various chokepoints to hold enemies out of their heartlands.\n\nEarly Game:\nThe clans are a totally independent nation and you have total freedom to choose who your enemies will be. Dunland starts on your eastern border with Isengard only a little further beyond them. South lies the great realm of Gondor and they will not be expecting an attack from the ‘rabble’ in Enedwaith. Crossing the Gwathló will bring you into contact with both Bree and the Northern Dúnedain and whilst the former may prove easy pickings, the latter will be a challenge to defeat. There are many rebel regions in the old lands of Cardolan and Minhiriath so you will have time to expand before you must declare war.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nThe clans rely heavily on ambush and guerrilla warfare: being neither the most numerous nor the strongest of people in Middle-earth. Their poor technological state and dis-unified nation leaves them outmatched by almost all other forces initially. The freefolk of Enedwaith do have one strength: their javelins. No other nation fields such deadly javelin throwers and even the walking immortals known as Eregion Smiths will fall like flowers before the hail of Enedwaith javelins.\n\nSpecial features:\n- Unite the Clans\n- Unconventional Recruitment\n- Athamor Reclaimation\n- Must unlock Cities and Strongholds\n- Ambush Specialists\n\nRare Units:\n- Tír Croícoille Cosantóirí (Athámor Reclaimed)\n- Báirseach Dragon Boats

A light was upon it for which his language had no name. All that he saw was shapely, but the shapes seemed at once clear cut, as if they had been first conceived and drawn at the uncovering of his eyes, and ancient as if they had endured forever. He saw no colour but those he knew, gold and white and blue and green, but they were fresh and poignant, as if he had at that moment first perceived them and made for them names new and wonderful. In winter here no heart could mourn for summer or for spring. No blemish or sickness or deformity could be seen in anything that grew upon the earth. On the land of Lórien, there was no stain." - Power of Lórien \n\nStarting Location:\nLothlórien is a well-protected realm with rivers surrounding the capital of Caras Galadhon. These are the key defensive locations for the golden wood and must be held at all costs. Your second city is outside the borders of the forest to the south but is offered protection from its remoteness. \nEarly Game:\nThe beginning of the campaign for Lórien will be dominated by the war with Dol Guldur. You can take the fight to the enemy and cut the head from the snake early or you can hold the Anduin against the poisonous shadow. In any event, you will have to deal with Dol Guldur early as there are few independent regions around the wood. The Dwarves of Khazad-dûm hold the mountain pass to the west and war with them is difficult to begin and ill-advised.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nLothlórien are defensive experts. They have excellent archers and their melee forces can hold any line against almost any foe. With various heavily specialised units throughout their ranks they have an answer for any scenario.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Yavanna’s Union\n- Slow Recruitment in Mountains\n\nRare Units:\n- Berio-i-Ngelaidh (Rejected Union)\n- Ents (Isengard)

This land race will dominate Dale’s starting game. Once lines have been drawn, you can cross the River Running and lay claim to the lands of Rhovanion before Dorwinion or Dol Guldur do. They have plenty of willing woodsmen and peasants who are eager to fight for Dale and can be used to engage the enemy whilst your superior archers finish them off. Additionally, Dale have one or two outstanding units that can turn battles in their favour and they have a nice variety of cavalry to help close the distance in the vast plains of the northeast.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Black Arrows (Armour Piercing)\n\nRare Units:\n- Athala Rangers (Rhovanion & Rhûn)\n- Elven & Dwarven Support (Dale)\n- Minas Ithil Guardians (Minas Morgul)\n- Ships

He has taken up with foul folk, with the Orcs. ... Worse than that: he has been doing something to them; something dangerous. For these Isengarders are more like wicked Men. They are wary of the White Wizard but not yet warlike. Dunland bays for blood and will likely attack the lesser men of Enedwaith allowing you to focus wholly on the Horselords. The towns between the Gap and the White Mountains are not currently beholden to any lord and are ripe for the taking in readiness for Rohan.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nSaruman, unlike Sauron, relies on quality over quantity and does not cheapen his armies with lesser Orcs: relying mostly on the fighting Uruk-hai. These formidable warriors wear heavy armour to keep them protected against any threat and this keen understanding of armour is also displayed in the multitude of anti-armour units Saruman fields for war.\n\nSpecial Features:\n Saruman of Many Colours (One Ring Mechanic)\n- Limited Cavalry\n\nRare Units:\n- Nazg-hai (Ring Acquired)\n- Olog-hai (Mordor Regions)\n- Trolls (Various Regions)\n- Guard of the Hand (Saruman’s Bodyguard)

Gundabad is now held by long forgotten kin who masquerade as ‘Melkor’s Chosen’. The foolish King of Goblin-town thinks himself above all Goblins and has declared independence. As such, the Goblins only hold Moria and Kala Gorge and stand alone.\n\nEarly Game:\nThe central location in Middle-earth gives the Goblins plenty of early opportunities. You can head down into Eregion and reclaim the long abandoned towns there or march north and bring Goblin-town to heel. In any event, it will not be long before one of your many enemies starts making trouble. The Northern Dúnedain start but a few regions away and heavily outmatch us. The Valesmen have been fighting against the Goblins for centuries and are strong foes. Lothlórien is with reach but only the foolish dare venture there!\n\nMilitary Overview:\nGoblins are the quintessential horde nation. Your forces are the weakest in Middle-earth but they are the most numerous. You will not defeat your foe through clever strategy or strength of arms but through sheer overwhelming numbers. The Goblins are not wholly devoted to the horde however and have developed excellent crossbows and deadly war machines.\n\nThe Balrog will not fight for the Goblins and does not feature in the campaign for them.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Khazad-dûm Expedition\n- Limited Cavalry and Elites\n\nRare Units:\n- Mountain Uruks (Mountain Regions)
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_GUNDABAD_TITLE}Gundabad
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_GUNDABAD_DESCR}Leader: King Mazog\nHeir: Marauder-Chief Ghurzog\nCapital: Mount Gundabad\n\n\n\n"In the Dwarvish traditions of the Third Age the names of the places where each of the Seven Ancestors had 'awakened' were remembered ... that of the ancestor of the Longbeards [Durin's Folk] ... had been Mount Gundabad (in origin a Khuzdul name), which was therefore revered by the Dwarves, and its occupation in the Third Age by the Orks of Sauron was one of the chief reasons for their great hatred of the Orks. ... Though these [places of awakening] were far sundered the Dwarves of different kindreds were in communication, and in the early ages often held assemblies of delegates at Mount Gundabad." - Description of Mount Gundabad \n\nStarting Location:\nThe paths to Mount Gundabad are few and dangerous. Only from the Vale can an enemy come upon the mountain with ease. This offers excellent protection to the isolated start of the Snow-orcs.\n\nEarly Game:\nThe long abandoned Dwarven castle of Burzakûl should be the first stop on the campaign for domination. There are however no other independent garrisons in the frozen north and inevitably, war with Erebor will come.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nThe Orcs of Gundabad are strong, uncharacteristically so for Orcs. The harsh conditions of the frozen tundra have shaped them into fierce warriors. They do not have the knowledge of armour as Isengard or Minas Morgul but they are certainly the finest of Melkor’s sons. They are also the least numerous however and their armies are not great in number. Snow-orcs are also keen sighted archers and are able to go toe-to-toe with Human marksmen.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Orc Subjugation\n- Scavenged Dwarven Armour\n\nRare Units:\n- Orc Avengers (Dwarven Mansions)\n- Snow Trolls (Various Regions)
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_SAXONS_TITLE}High Elves
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_SAXONS_DESCR}Leaders: High Lords Elrond and Círdan\nHeirs: Lords Elladan and Elrohir\nCapital: Mithlond\n\n\n\n"In all the days of the Third Age, after the fall of Gil-galad, Master Elrond abode in Imladris, and he gathered there many Elves, and other folk of wisdom and power from among all the kindred of Middle-earth, and he preserved through many lives of Men the memory of all that had been fair; and the house of Elrond was a refuge for the weary and the oppressed, and a treasury of good counsel and wise lore." - Nature of Imladris \n\nStarting Location:\nThe hidden valley is one of the most well-protected corners of Middle-earth. With only two points of entry and one being across a river, it is nigh-on unassailable! Whilst easily defended, you have no buffer zones to the enemy. Meanwhile, your holdings of Lindon and the Grey Havens in the west are far from your foes, and appear safe for the moment, and are sure to play a key role in your economy.\n\nEarly Game:\nThe Goblins of Moria are poised to assault Imladris at any moment from the southeast and the Angmarim are only a few provinces away to the north. There are numerous rebel settlements dotted around Rhudaur and the old lands of Hollin which are viable targets for Imladris however. There are also stout allies helping you in the early days such as the Northern Dúnedain, nestled safely in the Angle. Even with various independent towns to seize, it will not be long before you have to assault Moria or face early obliteration. In the west, you have no foes, but also must contend with your ally, Ered Luin for the few rebel regions.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nCenturies of brutal warfare against the shadow has made the remaining few Ñoldor the finest warriors on the face of Arda. Boasting Middle-earths’ best cavalry, infantry and archers, it is hard to see how any nation will hold back the High Elves. Unfortunately, the same conditions that have bred this excellent class of warrior have also caused the decline of their population and Imladris and Lindon cannot field large armies at any time. \n\nSpecial Features:\n- Glorfindel’s Uncloaking\n- Gwaith-i-Mirdain\n\nRare Units:\n- Eldarinwë Roquen (Imladris)\n- Eldarinwë Tirno (Imladris)\n-  Eregion Smiths (Eregion)\n- Mithlond Nobles (Mithlond)\n- Ships

Every region in Rhûn swears fealty to a different lord and they must be brought to heel. Once the lands are united, the wine-drinkers of Dorwinion will no doubt seek to turn our victory to their financial gain but it is advised to crush them swiftly, before the Avari get wind of our plans!\n\nMilitary Overview:\nRhûn is a land of diversity and each tribe fights in a distinct style. Once brought together, the united Easterling army is one of few weaknesses: crossbows first shower the enemy with armour piercing bolts; keen sighted rangers then unleash dragon fire on the field; heavily armoured infantry then locks the enemy in place whilst lighter and more manoeuvrable battalions harass and flank and; finally, the Lôke-Innas Rim thunder across the plains to finish off any stragglers or hardened targets.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Dragon Fire Arrows\n- Unite the Clans\n\nRare Units:\n- Süri'ut Chariots (Rhûn Regions)\n- Ships
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_SICILY_TITLE}Gondor
{IMPERIAL_CAMPAIGN_SICILY_DESCR}Leader: Steward Denethor II\nHeir: Captain-General Boromir\nCapital: Minas Tirith\n\n\n\n"Believe not that in the land of Gondor the blood of Númenor is spent, nor all its pride and dignity forgotten. By our valour the wild folk of the East are still restrained, and the terror of Morgul kept at bay; and thus alone are peace and freedom maintained in the lands behind us, bulwark of the West. But if the passages of the River should be won, what then?" - Gondor's strength and problem \n\nStarting Location:\nGondor has many natural defences against her enemies: the Anduin protects from the hordes of Mordor, Khand and Harad and; the Ered Nimrais gives cover from the nations of Enedwaith. She is also the largest nation in Middle-earth and the potential of her economic strength is certainly comforting.\n\nEarly Game:\nMordor dominates Gondor's entire campaign and the opening days of the War of the Ring are no different. There are few independent holds in these embattled lands and Mordor will arrive at the ruins of Osgiliath within a few years. Gondor must mobilise her enormous strength quickly and strike fast before Sauron can bring Harad, Khand and his own hordes to full production. All hope is not lost however for Prince Imrahil commands many battalions of elite warriors from Dol Amroth and he has been tasked with holding the Mouths of the Anduin.\n\nMilitary Overview:\nGondor is a nation of heavy infantry and skilled armourers, and even its lowest tier warriors boast well-made armour and arms. The famous White Tree is emblazoned on fine plate mail and the sight of a fully equipped Gondorian army arrayed on the field will give pause to any would-be foe. The cavalry of Gondor is few and far between however and is not the favoured method of combat for the Númenórean descendants.\n\nSpecial Features:\n- Fiefdom Recruitment\n- Fornost & Osgiliath Rebuilding\n\nRare Units:\n- Knights of the Silver Swan\n- Trebuchet (All Regions)\n- Ships

Their capital is easily defensible with a forest blocking access and two rivers protecting its western, eastern and southern approach. Amon Sûl on the other hand has only a few hills for minor protection but is otherwise standing adrift amongst the plains. Likewise, Metraith stands isolated in the south, watching over the Barrow Downs to the north.\n\nEarly Game:\nWhilst the Dúnedain start with few settlements, there are plenty of abandoned and independent towns in the lands of Cardolan, Arthedain and Rhudaur; including the great cities of Annúminas and Fornost. With the finest Human archers in Middle-earth for support, there are few enemies who can stand before a Dúnedain army. However, the long years and many wars have dwindled the Dúnedain population and their numbers are very few. Hope remains however on the shoulders of Aragorn son of Arathorn.
